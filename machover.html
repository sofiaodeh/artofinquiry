Episode 07

Tod Machover

Reimagining the Instrument

Tod Machover grew up with a pianist mother who sent him around the house to find objects, turn their sounds into stories, and draw a picture afterward so he could remember what he played. His father was an engineer who wanted anyone to be able to use a computer. Both ideas run through everything he has built since.

We talk about the hypercello he made for Yo-Yo Ma, a seven-foot construction kit for children that he set aside in favor of software, the opera he wrote for an orchestra of two, and already and not yet, his new piece for the Boston Symphony, where a live AI listens to every player and, for one section, the conductor stops conducting.

already and not yet

Tod's new piece for the Boston Symphony Orchestra, conducted by Kent Nagano and premiered at Symphony Hall in October 2026. Its three movements are Smash Up, Wake Up, and Stand Up. A live AI system responds to each of the roughly hundred musicians, and in the final section the conductor stops and the orchestra finds its own way.

  • Premiere and eventsMIT Media Lab

The hypercello

Built for Yo-Yo Ma, it measured how his bow moved and how the strings vibrated, read the emotion of the playing, and added sound around it, turning one cello into an orchestra. It premiered in Begin Again Again at Tanglewood in 1991.

  • Hyperstring Trilogyproject page

Toy Symphony

It began when Tod couldn't find a music teacher for his daughters like his mother had been. Children played Beatbugs, which pass a tapped rhythm from player to player, and squeezable Music Shapers, then wrote pieces for orchestras such as the BBC Symphony to perform.

  • The Music Toysproject page
  • The concertsproject page

The Big Thing and Hyperscore

The Big Thing was a seven-foot construction kit for composing, built by Maggie Orth. The bigger it grew, the more it felt like plumbing. Tod moved the idea into software, guided by Paul Klee's description of drawing as taking a line for a walk. In Hyperscore, children make short motives, give each a color, and draw lines that tell the music where to go.

  • Hyperscore and the Music Toysproject page

VALIS

Tod's first opera, based on the Philip K. Dick novel and commissioned by the Pompidou Center. When he couldn't get months of orchestra rehearsal, he wrote for two players, keyboard and percussion, and invented hyperinstruments to make them sound like many more. It was revived at MIT in 2023.

The Society of Mind

Marvin Minsky's theory of the mind, and Tod's reading recommendation for listeners. Minsky, a founder of artificial intelligence and one of the reasons Tod came to MIT, saw music as the most human activity there is.
