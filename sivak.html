Episode 09

Mark Sivak

Creativity Within Constraint

Mark Sivak is a teaching professor in Art and Design and in the Sherman Center for Engineering Entrepreneurship Education at Northeastern University, where he earned all three of his degrees. Before finishing his first, he helped scan a patient’s leg and 3D print an ankle brace shaped to it. His PhD turned stroke rehabilitation into games, played with a sensor glove or a bicycle wired into a virtual world.

We talk about why a good answer starts with a good question, what a haunted house teaches about immersion, and how Half-Life 2 keeps players inside its rules. Mark explains how VR can build empathy, how he taught hands-on prototyping over Zoom by building student projects in his garage and shipping them out, and why Northeastern’s new Performance and Extended Realities degree draws students from many fields.

The work we
talked about

An ankle brace shaped to one patient

As an undergraduate, Mark worked on scanning a patient’s leg and printing an ankle-foot orthosis to match it. The team knew it had to be biomedical and use 3D printing, and most of the creative work happened inside those limits. He came away believing that the more constraints you know, the better you understand the space you have to work in.

  • Patient specific ankle-foot orthoses using rapid prototypingJNER, 2011

Rehabilitation as a game

Relearning movement after a stroke is slow, and patients often feel they are making no progress. Mark’s PhD built virtual reality games to change that: a sensor glove for hand exercises and a stationary bicycle that measured how evenly a patient pedaled with their affected and unaffected sides. He worked with clinicians at Spaulding Rehabilitation Hospital in Boston.

  • Modular mechatronic system for stationary bicycles interfaced with virtual environment for rehabilitationJNER, 2014
  • Haptic system for hand rehabilitation integrating an interactive game with an advanced robotic deviceIEEE Haptics Symposium, 2010
  • Spaulding Rehabilitation HospitalBoston

Half-Life 2 and the rules of the world

A haunted house only scares you if you agree to be a good audience member. Mark sees the same agreement in games. In Half-Life 2, an enemy across a canyon hits the player every time, which quietly pushes them forward along the path the designers intended, while the world’s rules still hold. The designer’s job is to keep the rules they set.

  • Half-Life 2Valve, on Steam
  • Half-Life 2: Being Gordon FreemanMark’s essay in Well Played 1.0, free to read

Rekindle

A VR narrative game that reads the player’s face. Led by PhD student Hector Fan with Mark and Casper Harteveld, Rekindle asks players to live through other people’s memories and respond to them emotionally. One scene evokes the 2016 Pulse nightclub shooting, as a way to build understanding of the LGBTQ+ community through immersion.

  • Rekindle ignites player emotions for next-level VR immersionNortheastern Global News
  • Fostering Emotional Perspective-Takingresearch paper

VR for burn pain

Mark described a study in which burn patients played a VR game of throwing snowballs in an icy world during wound care, and reported less pain. That game is SnowWorld, developed at the University of Washington HITLab with Harborview Burn Center in Seattle by Hunter Hoffman and David Patterson.

  • VR pain control and SnowWorldUW HITLab
  • Virtual reality as an adjunctive pain control during burn wound care in adolescent patientsPain, 2000

Teaching from the garage

Mark taught Cornerstone, Northeastern’s first-year engineering course, where he told students that a good answer needs a good question. When classes moved online, he built his students’ projects in his garage and shipped them out, so teams could still prototype together over video.

  • Cornerstone of Engineeringcourse catalog
  • Educator Spotlight: Mark SivakNortheastern CATLR

Degrees in extended reality

Northeastern’s Performance and Extended Realities BS is only a year or two old, run jointly by Theatre, Music, and Art and Design. Mark also coordinates the MS in Extended Realities, which has concentrations as different as public history and marketing. Both bring in students who want to use the medium for whatever they care about.

  • Performance and Extended Realities, BSCAMD
  • Extended Realities, MSCAMD

Extra materials

Seventeen years earlier

In 2009, while working on his PhD, Mark wrote a close reading of Half-Life 2 for Well Played 1.0, a collection of essays on how individual games create meaning. Two years later he wrote about Legacy of Kain: Soul Reaver for Well Played 3.0. Both books are free to read under a Creative Commons license, and the Half-Life 2 essay covers the same ideas about player control he returns to in our conversation.

  • Half-Life 2: Being Gordon FreemanWell Played 1.0, 2009
  • Blocks, Planes, Drain, and Kain: Well Played for Legacy of Kain: Soul ReaverWell Played 3.0, 2011
